# Elsweyr, Summerset, Valenwood & the Thalmor (+ brief Hammerfell & High Rock), 3E 433 – c. 4E 40
_Researcher notes for Ashfall Ledger. Status tags: (no tag) = verified via cited source; [unverified]; [fan]; CONFLICT._

> Scope note: the lore for this region is **thin for 4E 1–22**. Most dated facts come from three
> sources: the UESP Fourth Era timeline, the in-game book *The Great War* (Legate Justianus Quintius,
> an Imperial soldier, so an Imperial viewpoint) and *Rising Threat, Vol. IV* (Lathenil of Sunhold,
> an anti-Thalmor Altmer writing a warning, so heavily biased). Much "daily life" detail below comes
> from Second Era (ESO) or Third Era sources. It is marked with its era where it matters, and it is
> safe to reuse only as "long-standing custom".

## 1. Timeline (dated facts, oldest → newest; focus 3E 427 – 4E 40)

### Background before the campaign
- **2E 309**: Keirgo of Anequina and Eshita of Pellitine marry, merging the two warring kingdoms into the Elsweyr Confederacy. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy
- **2E 311**: Rid-Thar-ri'Datta (the Mane) reveals the Riddle'Thar Epiphany at the Temple of Two-Moons Dance in Rawl'kha. This is the founding event of modern Khajiiti theology. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Rid-Thar-ri'Datta ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Temple_of_Two-Moons_Dance
- **2E 830**: heirs of the Camoran Dynasty fight over Valenwood's throne, and Summerset/Thalmor forces intervene for their own claimant, Camoran Anaxemes. The search summary's wording ("Thalmor were installed") may be loose. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood
- **c. 3E 413–433 ("last two decades of the Third Era")**: the moon-sugar trade flourishes in Elsweyr. Exact page not pinned: the search returned Lore:Elsweyr, Lore:Khajiit Cuisine and the IL *Infernal City Lore Notes: Elsweyr* together. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/infernal-city-lore-notes-elsweyr
- **3E 430 or 3E 433**: King Hlaalu Helseth of Morrowind renounces the slave trade or abolishes slavery, which frees Khajiit and Argonian slaves in law. Houses Dres and Hlaalu renounce the trade. The date is disputed; see §6. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Slavery ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:House_Dres ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Mournhold
- **Before 3E 433**: Thalmor agents have already formed close ties with certain Bosmeri factions in Valenwood. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/great-war
- **3E 433 (Oblivion Crisis)**: Summerset is hit hard. The Altmer "hold the line", but the Crystal Tower collapses from tremors caused by the Daedra. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Great_War_(book) ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/great-war
- **3E 433**: the Thalmor, until then a radical elitist movement, play a role in Summerset's defence. They later proclaim themselves saviours of the Altmer, claiming to have saved them from extinction "using deep and subtle magicks". The claim that they spread this by propaganda carries "citation needed" on UESP. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer
- **3E 433**: the walking city Falinesti roots itself for the first time in recorded history, in preparation for the crisis. The Wild Hunt breaks out across Valenwood to defend it, its first sighting in over 500 years. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_3rd_Edition/Valenwood
- **3E 433**: the Dremora clan of the Glenmoril region (Valenwood) are the ones who invaded Kvatch at the start of the Oblivion Crisis. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Glenmoril_(place)

### Fourth Era 1–40
- **4E 1**: the Fourth Era begins with no Emperor. High Chancellor Ocato (an Altmer of Firsthold, Summerset), the Elder Council and the Blades try to keep order. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ocato_of_Firsthold
- **4E 1–5 (campaign years)**: no specific events in Elsweyr, Summerset or Valenwood are recorded for 4E 1–9 in any result. Treat the region as "quiet, nominally Imperial, drifting". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era (absence of entries)
- **4E 6**: the Argonian invasion of Morrowind (Accession War), cross-reference only. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era
- **c. 4E 10**: per the Imperial Library timeline, Potentate Ocato is assassinated and the Stormcrown Interregnum begins. UESP's Elsweyr Confederacy page also uses "after the Stormcrown Interregnum began in 4E 10". CONFLICT with c. 4E 15 below. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/fourth-era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy
- **c. 4E 10 onward**: Elsweyr "once again found itself independent of the Empire, but was granted no peace". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy
- **4E 11–15**: the combined forces of Hammerfell and High Rock sack Orsinium, after the Oblivion Crisis and the destabilisation of the Third Empire. Many Orcs flee east into Skyrim. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Hammerfell ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:High_Rock ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Orsinium
- **4E 11–15**: the Imperial Legion escorts Orc survivors to the Hammerfell–Skyrim border, where they found the "fourth Orsinium". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Kingdoms_of_High_Rock ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Nord
- **c. 4E 15**: per UESP's timeline, Potentate Ocato is assassinated, "likely by Thalmor agents", and the Stormcrown Interregnum begins. For seven years the Elder Council is fractured by infighting over the Ruby Throne. UESP says the exact date is never given. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ocato_of_Firsthold ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Stormcrown_Interregnum
- **4E 18 or 4E 26**: Attrebus Mede is born (IL 4E 18 vs UESP 4E 26). This is a Long Chronicle date. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/fourth-era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era
- **4E 22**: the Thalmor seize total control of the Summerset Isles, overthrow the ancient Altmeri monarchy, and rename the province **Alinor** after its capital. Many outsiders still say "Summerset". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Summerset_Isles ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer
- **4E 22**: per *The Great War*, the Crystal Tower "was forced to give the Thalmor greater power and authority", and the Thalmor "use this discord as an opportunity" to overthrow Summerset's rulers. The new name "hearkens back to an earlier age before the ascendency of man". — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/great-war ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Great_War_(book)
- **c. 4E 22**: Titus Mede I captures the Imperial City, which ends the Stormcrown Interregnum and starts the Mede Dynasty. This is the endpoint of the Long Chronicle mode. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Mede_Dynasty
- **4E 29**: Valenwood's Imperial-friendly government is overthrown in a Thalmor-backed coup. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor
- **4E 29**: Imperial forces and Bosmer loyalists are caught off-guard and routed by better-prepared Bosmer and Thalmor troops. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/great-war
- **4E 29**: Valenwood's new government proclaims union with Alinor, creating the (third) Aldmeri Dominion. All contact with the Empire is severed. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Aldmeri_Dominion
- **4E 29 – 4E 48**: the Empire keeps supplying Bosmer rebels inside Valenwood. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood

### Later context (Long Chronicle and beyond, for rumours/foreshadowing only)
- **c. 4E 48**: the Mane is assassinated, by the Thalmor per the Mane article. Southern Elsweyr sinks into chaos, and an unknown figure declares himself **Potentate of Rimmen**. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Mane
- **c. 4E 48**: Thalmor agents harass, and possibly murder, refugees on Balfiera (High Rock) who are of mixed blood or otherwise "unclean". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Direnni
- **4E 43 or 4E 48**: Umbriel arrives (IL 4E 43 vs UESP 4E 48). — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/fourth-era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era
- **4E 98–100 (Void Nights)**: Masser and Secunda vanish. The Thalmor announce they restored the moons with Dawn Magicks. Whether they did is unresolved. The Khajiit hail them as saviours. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Void_Nights
- **c. 4E 100**: all Psijics are recalled and Artaeum vanishes again, coinciding with the Void Nights and a new wave of Dominion expansion. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Psijic_Order ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/guide-psijic-order
- **4E 115**: the Elsweyr Confederacy dissolves into Anequina and Pellitine, which become Dominion client states. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor
- **4E 168**: Titus Mede II takes the throne. Hammerfell is still an Imperial province. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Hammerfell
- **4E 171–175 (Great War)**: the Dominion invades through western Cyrodiil and Hammerfell. **Early 4E 173**: a Forebear army from Sentinel breaks the Aldmeri siege of the Crown city of Hegathe, reconciling the factions. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Great_War ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Hegathe ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Sentinel
- **4E 175**: the White-Gold Concordat bans Talos worship, disbands the Blades, and cedes much of southern Hammerfell. Hammerfell refuses it and fights on about five more years to become united and independent. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:White-Gold_Concordat ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Crowns
- **4E 180**: Second Treaty of Stros M'Kai ends Hammerfell's war [unverified]. — Source: should live at https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Hammerfell
- **4E 188**: paranoid King Barynia leaves Wayrest exposed, and it is sacked by Breton corsairs. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:High_Rock
- **4E 200–201**: the Psijic Quaranir intervenes at the College of Winterhold. Khajiit caravans under Ri'saad are barred from Skyrim's cities. Thalmor Justiciars enforce the Talos ban. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Psijic_Order ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Caravans ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Thalmor_Justiciar

## 2. Places (status in 4E 4, 4E 5, 4E 6, and later if known)

### Elsweyr (province)
- Controlling faction / ruler: the Elsweyr Confederacy (Anequina in the north plus Pellitine in the south, united since 2E 309), under the Mane at Torval as spiritual and temporal leader. In 4E 4–6 it is nominally Imperial. It becomes de facto independent from c. 4E 10. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Torval
- The name and the ruling Mane in 4E 4–6 are never given. The Mane killed c. 4E 48 may or may not be the same individual [unverified]. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Mane
- Economy: southern Elsweyr has an agrarian aristocracy exporting saltrice and moon sugar. The north is largely nomadic. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_3rd_Edition/Sugar_and_Blood
- Moon sugar is Elsweyr's chief export, and it is smuggled out both raw and refined (as skooma). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar
- What happens 4E 4–6: nothing recorded. Later: independence from c. 4E 10, the Mane's murder and a Rimmen Potentate c. 4E 48, Thalmor "saviours" after 4E 100, and partition in 4E 115. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy
- The province has eight major cities. The four confirmed in results are Rimmen, Riverhold, Dune and Corinthe, plus Senchal and Torval. Orcrest is the probable seventh [unverified]. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Senchal
- The Sixteen Kingdoms of old Elsweyr included Riverhold and Corinthe as royal seats. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Riverhold

### Senchal (Pellitine, south coast)
- Controlling faction: Confederacy city. It is one of the eight major cities and one of the largest ports in southern Tamriel. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Senchal
- Economy & goods: a haven for pirates, thieves and the black market. Historically it was the main port of the drug trade. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Senchal
- Around the turn of the Third Era (wording as given) it shifted "from the largest port in the drug trade to a coastal resort for wealthy Khajiit". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Senchal ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:History_of_Senchal:_An_Overview
- **Black Kiergo**: Senchal's slum district, with illicit moon-sugar dens lining the streets. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Black_Kiergo
- **Topal Bay**: the trading waters beside Senchal. UESP uses "Topal Bay", not "Bay of Senchal". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Senchal
- 4E 4–6: no specific events. Atmosphere: resort villas over slum dens, pirates in the harbour. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Senchal

### Torval (Pellitine, Tenmar Forest)
- Seat of the Mane, the Khajiit spiritual and temporal leader. Torval is a major city in the Tenmar Forest. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Torval
- The Torval Curiata (a sub-faction, ESO-era attestation) enforce the epiphanies of Riddle'Thar and fight heretics. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Torval
- Moon-sugar cane is native to the Tenmar Forest. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar

### Rawl'kha
- Home of the Temple of Two-Moons Dance ("House of the Dance"), perhaps the most important temple in modern Khajiiti theology, where the 2E 311 Epiphany happened. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Temple_of_Two-Moons_Dance

### Rimmen (Anequina, "the Rim")
- The modern capital of Anequina, in north-eastern Elsweyr near the Cyrodiil border. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Rimmen
- Atmosphere: ancient canals and aqueducts irrigate the land, making it a green oasis in the badlands. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Rimmen
- An in-game book says the price of moon sugar is fixed by the office of the Potentate, and selling more than two pounds is punishable by execution. This is probably Second Era (ESO) Rimmen; the era is not pinned. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Cuisine
- Later: c. 4E 48 an unknown figure declares himself Potentate of Rimmen. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy

### Riverhold (northern Elsweyr)
- The river-city through which travellers from Cyrodiil enter northern Elsweyr. It is known for crowded markets and was one of the original Sixteen Kingdoms. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Riverhold

### Dune (north-west, Reaper's March)
- Near the Valenwood border, in the Reaper's March region that links Valenwood's jungles to Elsweyr's plains. A natural Elsweyr–Valenwood trade-route node. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Category:Lore-Places-Tamriel-Elsweyr
- Reaper's March hosts moon-sugar plantations outside the cane's native Tenmar range. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar

### Corinthe (also "Corinth"; heart of the province)
- On the northern edge of the Tenmar Forest, once a seat of one of the Sixteen Kingdoms. Its Khajiit were known as master carpenters and lumberers. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Corinthe

### Khenarthi's Roost
- An island with moon-sugar plantations. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar

### Summerset Isles / Alinor (province)
- Controlling faction: in 4E 4–6, the ancient Altmeri monarchy, with the Sapiarchs of the Crystal Tower as the learned authority and the Thalmor rising. From 4E 22 the Thalmor rule and the province is renamed Alinor. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Summerset_Isles ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor
- 4E 4–6 situation: the Crystal Tower lies collapsed since 3E 433, and the Thalmor are gaining credit as defenders of the isles. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Great_War_(book)
- The Altmer have perfected their cuisine in isolation "without much interference from the outside races". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Cuisine
- Artaeum (Psijic Order isle) is presumably present in 4E 4–6, since its recorded later vanishing is c. 4E 100 [unverified inference]. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Psijic_Order

### Alinor (city)
- The capital that gives the province its 4E 22 name. Vineyards lie outside the city walls, and the **Oleander Coast Winery** is just beyond its northern gate. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Alinor_(city) ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Summerset_Isles

### Shimmerene
- The main trade port of the isles, "where most of the trade from overseas comes" (ESO-era). It serves an "ambrosial" Golden Pear Punch. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/General:ESO_Live_Summerset_Story_and_Lore ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Cuisine

### Russafeld
- Called "the birthplace of wine in our Isles". Its vineyards run across much of the land between Rellenthil and Shimmerene. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/our-blessed-isles-guide
- Wines: "bridal-veil" (white), "roseheart" (red) and Russafeld Red. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Cuisine

### Cloudrest
- Gives its name to the brewed **Golden Altmer ale**. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Cuisine

### Lillandril
- Hosts the College of Sapiarchs, which includes a Sapiarch devoted to food and wine. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Summerset_Isles ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Cuisine

### Firsthold (Auridon)
- Described as the oldest city in the isles, on Auridon's northern point. It is the home of Ocato, Potentate of the Empire until his murder. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Summerset_Isles ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ocato_of_Firsthold

### Skywatch / Errinorne Isle / Vulkhel Guard (Auridon)
- Errinorne Isle near Skywatch was used by the ancient Aldmer as a mercantile hub. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Summerset_Isles
- Vulkhel Guard exports seafood and fish, its main products, to Valenwood, which values them highly, and gets Bosmer crafts in return (ESO-era). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Vulkhel_Guard

### Crystal Tower
- Kept by the Sapiarchs. It holds "Transparent Law" at its top, and scholars theorise the crystal absorbs the Altmeri drive for perfection and reflects it back as protection for the isle. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Crystal_Tower
- Historically, entry required a pair of Tower Sentinels bearing the two Resolute Diamonds. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Sages_of_the_Crystal_Tower
- Collapsed from Daedric tremors in 3E 433. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/great-war

### Valenwood (province)
- Controlling faction: in 4E 4–6, an Imperial-friendly Bosmer government with Thalmor-linked factions in the background. From 4E 29 it is part of the Aldmeri Dominion. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood
- The current capital is not known for certain; an older talk-page guess is Falinesti. Elden Root was the Dominion capital in the Second Era (ESO). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Valenwood ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elden_Root
- Economy: the Bosmer depend heavily on trade, especially for timber imports. Their own exports are hides, river pearls, and finger-bone charms made from the magically charged hands of dead wizards. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer
- When at odds with Colovia or other partners, Bosmer reportedly turn to piracy or banditry. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer
- Falinesti, Silvenar, Haven, Archen, Eldenroot and Woodhearth grew from local trading posts into powers in their own right. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_3rd_Edition/Valenwood
- 4E 4–6: no recorded events. Later: the 4E 29 coup, and loyalist rebels supplied by the Empire until 4E 48. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood

### Falinesti (the walking city)
- Built on a giant graht-oak. It was Valenwood's historic capital and the Camoran seat, migrating between seasonal sites, and it vanished c. 2E 382. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Falinesti ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood
- It rooted for the first time in 3E 433 and has stayed rooted at its former summer site, in northern Malabal Tor. The site is disputed; see §6. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood
- Atmosphere for 4E 4–6: a "walking city" that no longer walks, its people still on edge after the Wild Hunt. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_3rd_Edition/Valenwood

### Elden Root (Grahtwood)
- A rooted, non-migrating city on the Elden Tree (a graht-oak). It became the Camoran seat after Falinesti vanished. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elden_Root

### Arenthia / Marbruk / Silvenar
- These appear in the 4E 29 coup search results, but no 4E content was returned. Arenthia is a Reaper's March town on the Elsweyr–Valenwood border [unverified]. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Arenthia ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Marbruk ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Silvenar

### Hammerfell (brief)
- In 4E 4–6 it is presumably still an Imperial province. That is an inference: it is attested as Imperial in 4E 168, and nothing records it seceding earlier. It is plagued by Crown–Forebear infighting, though how peaceful they were is disputed; see §6. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Hammerfell ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Forebears
- Forebears descend from the Ra Gada warrior class and are cosmopolitan. Crowns descend from Yokudan nobility, shun outsiders and preserve ancestral customs. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Forebears ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Crowns
- **Sentinel**: in the north-western hills overlooking the Iliac Bay. At one point it controlled a third of all Iliac Bay trade; the era of that claim is unclear in the result. Its principal street is a vast marketplace from the harbour to the badlands gate (Pocket Guide, 1st ed.). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Sentinel ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_1st_Edition/Hammerfell
- **Rihad**: a Forebear port city, and in the Fourth Era one of the main ports on the East Empire Company's western lines. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Rihad
- 4E 11–15: joins High Rock in sacking Orsinium. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Hammerfell

### High Rock (brief)
- Bretons rose through commerce and well-placed villages. The only true cities lie along the Iliac Bay, whose kingdoms prospered on trade flowing to the Bjoulsae River. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_1st_Edition/High_Rock ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/pocket-guide-empire-third-edition-sons-and-daughter-direnni-west-high-rock
- **Wayrest** prospered after Orsinium was razed in 1E 980. It is Orsinium's natural rival. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/high-rock
- **Direnni Tower / Balfiera**: its hereditary castellans are High Elves, the only known Elven ruling family left in human lands. That makes it a Thalmor interest; see the c. 4E 48 harassment above. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Direnni
- **Evermor, Daggerfall**: no Fourth Era information found. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:High_Rock (absence)
- **Orsinium**: sacked 4E 11–15 by Breton and Redguard kingdoms acting together. A rebuilt Orsinium between Skyrim and Hammerfell was guarded by the Seventh and Fifteenth Legions; whether that refers to this rebuild or an earlier one is unclear in the result. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Iliac_Bay ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Orsinium

## 3. Factions & people (who, stance, fate)
- **Thalmor (origins)**: originally an obscure Altmeri council within Summerset's government, tasked with safeguarding Altmeri heritage. They grew into a governing and enforcement body during the First and Second Dominions. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor
- **Thalmor (ideology)**: a "shadowy cabal" dedicated to Elven supremacy, a militarist regime aiming at Mer superiority over Men. A talk page notes the game text never uses exactly these terms; the label is inferred from dialogue. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Thalmor ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim_talk:Thalmor
- **Thalmor stance in 4E 4–6**: a radical, elitist faction riding post-Crisis prestige inside Summerset. They are not yet rulers (until 4E 22) and are already cultivating Bosmer factions. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/great-war
- **Ceythalmor**: a secret Thalmor sub-organisation that attempted a coup to assassinate Queen Ayrenn in the First (Second-Era) Dominion. It is a precedent for Thalmor intrigue, not tied to 4E. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor
- Thalmor doctrine (Skyrim-era): "Talos was only a man" and does not deserve a place in the pantheon. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Thalmor
- **Sapiarchs of the Crystal Tower**: Summerset's scholar-authorities. Per the Great War page they "ceded" power to the Thalmor. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Great_War
- **Altmeri monarchy**: overthrown 4E 22. No monarch's name was found [unverified]. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer
- **Ocato of Firsthold**: Altmer High Chancellor and later Potentate, who held the Empire together after 3E 433. Assassinated c. 4E 10/15, widely believed by the Thalmor. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ocato_of_Firsthold ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Septim_Empire
- **Lathenil of Sunhold**: Altmer author of *Rising Threat*, the main in-world source accusing the Thalmor of Ocato's murder. He is an unreliable, polemical narrator. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Rising_Threat,_Vol._IV ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/rising-threat
- **Legate Justianus Quintius**: Imperial soldier, author of *The Great War*, the main source for the 4E 22 and 4E 29 events. Imperial bias. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Great_War_(book)
- **The Mane**: the Khajiit spiritual and temporal leader at Torval. A Mane can be born only under a rare alignment of Masser and Secunda when, by legend, a third moon appears ("dark eclipse"). A Moon Herald guides aspirants on a quest to the Two Moons, and the sole survivor becomes Mane. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Mane ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit
- **Lunar (Two-Moons) Clergy**: run the Temples of Two-Moons Dance. Members are Moon Priests and the leader is the Moon Bishop. Moon Bishops strictly control ritual skooma use in certain ceremonies. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Temple_of_Two-Moons_Dance ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar
- **Bosmer pro-Thalmor factions**: tied to Thalmor agents before 3E 433. They take power in the 4E 29 coup. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/great-war
- **Bosmer loyalists / Imperial-backed government**: overthrown 4E 29. Rebels keep fighting with Imperial supply until 4E 48. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood
- **Camoran Dynasty**: the historic rulers of Valenwood, seated at Falinesti and then Elden Root. Oblivion wrongly presented them as Altmer; they are Bosmer. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elden_Root ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Altmer_Names
- **The Wild Hunt**: a Bosmer transformation and rampage that "results from the Green Pact". It was seen in 3E 433 for the first time in over 500 years. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood:_A_Study ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_3rd_Edition/Valenwood
- **Psijic Order**: on Artaeum. Their periodic withdrawals are believed to be a retreat from chaotic periods. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Psijic_Order
- **Crowns & Forebears (Hammerfell)**: the rival Redguard factions described above. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Forebears
- **Direnni castellans (Balfiera)**: the last Elven ruling family in human lands. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Direnni
- **Khajiit caravans** (later context): kept outside Skyrim's city walls in 4E 201 because of a smuggling reputation; some traded skooma and Sleeping Tree sap. They are led by Ri'saad of Elsweyr. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Caravans ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ri'saad

## 4. Goods, trade & laws relevant to this area (what is produced, what is contraband, tariffs, currency)

### Moon sugar
- A spice from Elsweyr with modest magical properties, and also a strong narcotic. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar
- Refined from cane grasses of Elsweyr's coasts and estuaries. The cane is native to the Tenmar Forest, with plantations on Khenarthi's Roost and in Reaper's March. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar
- Southern Elsweyr's fertile soil supports sugarcane and saltrice plantations. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Cuisine
- **Legality**: illegal throughout the Third Empire, including Third Era Morrowind, but often smuggled. The classic route is through caves on Morrowind's Bitter Coast. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Senchal
- In Morrowind (3E 427 game mechanics), possession is not technically reported as a crime, but most vendors refuse to trade with a carrier. Khajiit merchants are the exception. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Moon_Sugar ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Crime
- Khajiit eat it in some form daily and are relatively resistant to it. Humans and Mer feel stronger effects, and over-consumption can addict even Khajiit. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Sugar
- The "sacred, harmless cooking ingredient" framing comes from a satirical, biased report by an Imperial Trade Service agent. No neutral source calls raw moon sugar sacred. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon-Sugar:_A_Report
- **4E 4–6 legal status**: no source addresses moon-sugar law after the Septim line ended. The Imperial ban presumably stays on the books but enforcement collapses with the Interregnum; Elsweyr itself is independent from c. 4E 10 [unverified]. — Source: none found

### Skooma
- A hallucinatory narcotic refined (easily distilled) from moon sugar. It is extremely addictive and leaves users swinging between delusional euphoria and lethargy. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moon_Worship_among_the_Cat-Men
- Long-term use is said to cause permanent confusion, mental deterioration and loss of teeth. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma
- Skooma cut with spoiled (mouldy) moon sugar is cheaper, "regrettably common", and can kill. Withdrawal can provoke violence. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma
- Addiction is commonly thought incurable, but cures are recorded through alchemy and through ending the addict's self-hatred. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma
- **Banned throughout history by both the Aldmeri Dominion and the Third Empire.** This matters for a Thalmor-ruled Summerset after 4E 22 and the Dominion after 4E 29. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma
- It is typically smuggled together with moon sugar. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma
- In-world testimony: *Confessions of a Dunmer Skooma-Eater*. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/confessions-dunmer-skooma-eater
- In-game mechanics (no addiction in Morrowind; it restores Stamina in Skyrim) contradict the lore. Use the lore. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Skooma ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Skooma

### Other Elsweyr goods
- Saltrice (export crop of the southern aristocracy). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Cuisine
- Timber and carpentry from Corinthe ("master carpenters and lumberers"). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Corinthe
- Sleeping Tree sap, an illicit good of Khajiit caravans (attested 4E 201). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Caravans

### Summerset goods
- Wines: Summerset Hills Wine (a white "renowned for its overpowering subtlety"), Oleander Coast Wine (so rare it is drunk from a goblet holding a fresh sapphire garnish), Russafeld bridal-veil (white) and roseheart (red), and Russafeld Red. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Cuisine
- No canon "Alto wine" entry was found; the search for it turned up nothing. Do not use the name without checking. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Cuisine (absence)
- Golden Altmer ale (Cloudrest) and Golden Pear Punch (Shimmerene). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Cuisine
- Seafood and fish from Auridon, exported to Valenwood (ESO-era). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Vulkhel_Guard
- Moonstone (Elven armour metal) and malachite (glass) as High Elf crafting materials [unverified]. — Source: should live at https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moonstone

### Valenwood goods
- Hides, river pearls, and finger-bone charms (from dead Bosmer wizards' hands). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer
- Composite horn bows, "the finest in Tamriel". Traditional bows are horn and sinew, with bone arrows fletched with bird feathers. Claimed effects of different bone types failed Imperial trials. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Common_Arms_of_Valenwood
- Rotmeth (fermented-meat liquor), jagga (fermented pig's milk) and Sun's Dusk Ale. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Alcoholic_Beverages ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/dance-fire-v7
- **Imports**: Valenwood depends on timber imports. Under the Green Pact, Bosmer may use woodcrafts made by other races and may sell Valenwood timber as long as a non-Bosmer collects it. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pocket_Guide_to_the_Empire,_1st_Edition/Aldmeri_Dominion

### Hammerfell / High Rock goods (brief)
- Alik'r spices are rare and pricey, kept in special decanters or handmade jars. Hammerfell hot peppers are prized by overseas cooks. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Redguard_Cuisine
- Pomegranate wine is Alik'r's most popular wine, made with a pomegranate distiller. There are also Sentinel cutlery and Alik'r spice grinders (Second Era items). — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/items-hammerfell
- Redguard dhows are by far the most common vessels in Hammerfell, carrying goods and people. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Sea_Transportation
- No Breton or Wayrest wine reference was found in the High Rock guide chapters. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/pocket-guide-empire-third-edition-sons-and-daughter-direnni-west-high-rock (absence)

### Laws, slavery, trade politics
- Morrowind's abolition (Helseth, 3E 430/433) appears to have ended slavery in Morrowind. Khajiit slave-taking is therefore illegal in 4E 4–6 Morrowind as far as the lore says. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Slavery
- From 4E 29, the Dominion "severed all contact" with the Empire. That means no legal Imperial trade with Valenwood and Alinor from then on; any trade is smuggling. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era
- Currency: Septims in Imperial lands [unverified for 4E 4–6]. Dominion gold coinage in 4E 201 [unverified]. — Source: should live at https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Septim

## 5. Culture & daily life (food, drink, religion, naming conventions with examples, festivals, slang)

### Khajiit: moons, furstocks, faith
- Khajiit form depends on the phases of the moons, Masser (greater) and Secunda (lesser), known to Khajiit as Jone and Jode, at birth. The governing force is the Lunar Lattice (ja-Kha'jay). A Khajiit keeps its birth form for life. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Masser ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Secunda
- Khajiit hold the moons to be the remains of Lorkhan [unverified]. — Source: should live at https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Masser
- Birth-phase table examples: Cathay = Masser waxing / Secunda full; Suthay = both new; Alfiq = Masser waning / Secunda full; Dagi = Masser waning / Secunda new. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/khajiit-physiology-phases-and-forms
- **Cathay / Cathay-raht**: bipedal. Cathay-raht are like Cathay but bigger, "more agile than a werewolf", and great warriors. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Cathay-raht
- **Dagi / Dagi-raht**: Dagi are among the smallest furstocks. Dagi-raht are taller and prefer to live in trees. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit
- **Ohmes / Ohmes-raht**: Ohmes are said to be the only furstock without the "blessing of fur". Ohmes-raht are easily mistaken for Men or Mer, though they look more human. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ohmes ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit
- **Pahmar / Pahmar-raht**: giant furstocks. Pahmar-raht are called the greatest Khajiit generals and strategists. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit
- **Senche / Senche-raht**: quadrupedal. Senche-raht are larger and slower than Senche. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit
- **Suthay / Suthay-raht**: Suthay-raht are bipedal, good jumpers, agile and sneaky with a bold spirit. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit
- **Alfiq**: diminutive, magically gifted, quadrupedal and housecat-like. Whether they can speak is disputed; see §6. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Alfiq
- **Riddle'Thar** (Two-Moons Dance): the Khajiit cosmic-order deity, "more a set of guidelines by which to live than a single entity". Its followers also worship the Lunar Lattice. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Riddle'Thar ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Temple_of_Two-Moons_Dance
- **Alkosh** is a pre-ri'Datta Anequinine deity figure. His temple, Sunspire, lost significance after the Epiphany. **Khenarthi** is a blessed spirit and the antithesis of **Namiira**. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Temple_of_Two-Moons_Dance ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Secrets_of_the_Riddle'Thar
- "Clan Mother tales" are mentioned once (the Mane "offered us more than Clan Mother tales — he offered us grace"). Their meaning is unexplained. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Epistle_on_the_Spirits_of_Amun-dro

### Khajiit speech
- Ta'agra is the Khajiit language. Most natives also speak Tamrielic and often slip Ta'agra words into it. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ta'agra
- Khajiiti Tamrielic favours the passive voice and third-person self-reference ("this one"), and turns questions into statements ending in "yes?" or "no?". Example: "You have traveled far to see this one, yes?" — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ta'agra
- Ta'agra probably has no first-person pronoun, which may explain the third-person habit. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ta'agra
- The habit is not universal. Zhasim, a Khajiit raised by Orcs, does not use these patterns and finds them odd. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Zhasim

### Khajiit naming (honorific prefixes)
- **Ri'**: the highest title, for chieftains, kings, leaders of tribes or cities, and the Mane. Rarely awarded. Example: Ri'saad (caravan patriarch). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/interviews-3-booksellers ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ri'saad
- **Ra'**: an esteemed leader of soldiers, trade or government. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/lang-taagra ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names
- **Dar**: perhaps the most ancient title, for those clever with hands and wits. Its root meaning is "thief", but "this should not alarm you". It is the only title known to be used as a suffix. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/interviews-3-booksellers ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Khajiit_Names
- **Do'**: a warrior distinguished in battle or duels. Rarely used today except by the Mane's guards and martial-arts masters. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/lang-taagra ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names
- **Jo'**: respected physicians, scholars and mages, and also adopted by charlatans and mountebanks. It is sometimes seen as a suffix. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/khajiiti-honorifics ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Khajiit_Names
- **Ma' / M'**: child or apprentice. Normally endearing unless used in contempt, and slang for "virgin". Because an apprentice can be any age, it causes jokes. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/interviews-3-booksellers
- **J'**: a bachelor or young adult. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names
- **S'**: the most common prefix, simply "adult". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names
- **-dro / -dra**: elder, grandfather or grandmother, patriarch or matriarch, a mark of respect. A joke definition adds "marketplace slowpokes". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiiti_Honorifics ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/interviews-3-booksellers
- Only one title per name. Stacking titles (e.g. "S'basha-Jo") is a sign of ignorance or pride. — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/interviews-3-booksellers
- Caveat: these meanings come mainly from an out-of-game 2001 developer piece (*Interview With Three Booksellers*). They are community-accepted, not in-game canon. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Khajiit_Names
- More name examples: Keirgo, Eshita, Rid-Thar-ri'Datta, Ahkari, Zhasim. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Ahkari

### Khajiit food
- Moon sugar is used as a spice in almost every dish. Other dishes include Pellitine rice cooked with tomato and a rice dish with bananas. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Cuisine

### Altmer culture & naming
- The Altmer pursue cultural perfection and venerate lineage, stressing purity of form and continuity of tradition. The Scrolls of Praxis serve as a moral and ceremonial code. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer
- An allegation that the Altmer practised infanticide to remove "impurities" comes from outsider literature whose impartiality is questioned. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer
- Arena/Daggerfall male names are prefix + suffix from 12 each. Examples of prefixes: Core, Kala, Soli; suffixes: -dalf, -las, -ron. So: Kaladalf, Solilas, Coreron (constructed examples). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Names
- Female prefixes: Andra, Ca, Cymba, Kori, Lili, Lora, Mor, Psy, Saur, Sha, Valli, Zeno. Suffixes include -sara and -lia (e.g. Lorasara, Valli-lia, constructed). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Names
- Family names are rare and seldom displayed. Known surname prefixes include High-, Storm-, Throm-. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Names
- Later games use hand-picked names. Canon examples: Ocato, Lathenil, Ondolemar, Ayrenn, Iachesis. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer_Names ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ocato_of_Firsthold ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Ondolemar ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Iachesis
- Altmer often bear place-based epithets ("of Firsthold", "of Sunhold") as seen in canon names. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ocato_of_Firsthold ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Rising_Threat,_Vol._IV

### Bosmer culture & naming
- **Green Pact**: Bosmer may not harm Valenwood's woodlands and must eat only meat. Fallen fruit is not a breach. Y'ffre's (or the Green Lady's) clarification restates it as "do not harm living plants". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Green_Pact
- Most Bosmer accept dairy, honey, eggs, mushrooms and insects; only ultra-zealots object. Bosmer do not farm. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Green_Pact ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Truth_About_the_Green_Pact
- **Rotmeth**: fermented meat, stirred for weeks in sunlit clearings. The "best" is rested for years. No wedding can happen without it. Fans call it "pleasantly gamey and salty"; detractors say it tastes of "foot fungus and calcite". — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Alcoholic_Beverages ; https://www.imperial-library.info/content/on-rotmeth ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer_Cuisine
- **Jagga**: boiled pig's-milk cream, stirred for days, thickened with dried curd shards and fermented for 3 weeks. The **Dubious Camoran Throne** is a dessert cocktail of jagga, rotmeth and puréed sweetbreads. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer_Cuisine
- An Imperial traveller found rotmeth rancid and Sun's Dusk Ale too gamey, and by the time he learned jagga was fermented pig's milk "he was too far gone to care". — Source: https://www.imperial-library.info/content/dance-fire-v7
- The Bosmer are the best archers in Tamriel, said to "snatch and release arrows in one continuous motion" and rumoured to have invented the bow. Youths join hunting parties by 14. Long-distance archers are called **Jaqspurs**. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer
- Male names: prefixes An, Ara, Ar, Co, Elis, Karo, Lego, Li, Pali, Ria, Sil, Ta plus suffixes such as -cher, -dell, -gan, -rim (e.g. Silgan, Aradell, constructed). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer_Names
- Female names: prefixes such as Cas-, Min- plus -dra, -nia (e.g. Casnia, Mindra, constructed). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer_Names
- Surnames, when used, are nature compounds (Fern/Moss/Willow + brook/dale/wood, giving Fernwood, Willowbrook). Most Bosmer go by clan-given nicknames and bynames instead. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer_Names/Family ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer_Names
- Canon example: Camoran Anaxemes (a dynastic name). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Valenwood

### Redguard (brief)
- Port cities bring in outside goods that vary the diet, and Alik'r spices are luxury items. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Redguard_Cuisine

## 6. Conflicts & uncertainties (each with both sources)
- CONFLICT (**how the Thalmor took Summerset, 4E 22**): a coup d'état overthrowing the monarchy (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Thalmor) **vs** the Sapiarchs of the Crystal Tower "ceded" power (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Great_War) **vs** the Crystal Tower "was forced to give" the Thalmor more power, which they then used to overthrow the rulers (https://www.imperial-library.info/content/great-war). Default reading: a staged takeover ending in a coup.
- CONFLICT (**Thalmor saved Summerset?**): the Thalmor claim credit for saving the Altmer with "deep and subtle magicks" (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Altmer) **vs** UESP tags the propaganda-campaign claim "citation needed", and *The Great War* says the Altmer held the line but the Tower fell anyway (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Great_War_(book)).
- CONFLICT (**when Ocato died and the Interregnum began**): c. 4E 15 (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Ocato_of_Firsthold) **vs** 4E 10 (https://www.imperial-library.info/content/fourth-era ; and UESP's own https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr_Confederacy). UESP says the exact date is never given (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Stormcrown_Interregnum).
- CONFLICT (**who killed Ocato**): the Thalmor, per *Rising Threat IV* by the hostile Lathenil (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Rising_Threat,_Vol._IV), repeated as "likely" or "believed" (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Fourth_Era ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Septim_Empire) **vs** a UESP talk page asking for real evidence and suggesting the claim be softened (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Fourth_Era). A forum suggestion that a Nibenese lord did it is fan speculation, not canon.

## 7. Game hooks (SUGGESTIONS ONLY — events, rumors, price effects, route changes, encounters, codex ideas)
- SUGGESTION: **Rumour pair "Who saved Summerset?"**: one rumour says "The Thalmor's deep and subtle magicks saved the isles"; the rival says "the Tower fell anyway, and the Thalmor were only loud". Both are sourced (Lore:Altmer vs *The Great War*).
- SUGGESTION: **Crystal Tower rubble**: from 4E 4, Summerset sells "Crystal shard (reliquary)" as a high-value curio. Present it as invented flavour, not a canon item.
- SUGGESTION: **Moon-sugar run**: Senchal (Black Kiergo dens, cheapest) → Topal Bay → Bitter Coast caves (Morrowind, highest price). Most Morrowind vendors refuse to trade while you carry it, except Khajiit, matching the Morrowind mechanic.
- SUGGESTION: **Red Year price spike (4E 5)**: with Morrowind in chaos, Bitter Coast smuggling coves become risky. Raise Elsweyr→Morrowind moon-sugar margins and the chance of losing cargo.
- SUGGESTION: **Mouldy-sugar skooma**: a cheap skooma variant at Senchal with a hidden chance to "kill a buyer", causing a reputation loss. Based on Lore:Skooma's spoiled-sugar note.
- SUGGESTION: **Riverhold gate market**: the cheapest entry point for Cyrodiilic goods into Elsweyr. Dune is the Valenwood–Elsweyr transfer node for hides, pearls and horn bows.
- SUGGESTION: **Valenwood timber paradox**: Valenwood imports timber. A player hauling Cyrodiilic lumber to Valenwood gets premium prices, and a codex entry explains the Green Pact exemption.
- SUGGESTION: **Rotmeth wedding demand**: a random "Bosmer wedding season" event spikes rotmeth demand in Falinesti and Elden Root.
- SUGGESTION: **Falinesti rooted**: a codex note for 4E 4 that the walking city stands still for the first time in history, with Wild Hunt rumours lingering from 3E 433.
