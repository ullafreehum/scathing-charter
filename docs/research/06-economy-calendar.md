## 1. Timeline (dated facts, oldest → newest; focus 3E 427 – 4E 40)
- **c. Middle Merethic / early 1E (Ayleid era)**: the Ayleid version of Midyear was 57 days long and lasted "all across summer" until Pelinal Whitestrake split it. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Calendar
- **Same period**: UESP's Pelinal article credits him with "somehow erasing an entire month from the elvish calendar." (The exact year, c. 1E 242–243, is [unverified].) — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pelinal_Whitestrake
- **Late 2E onward (Third Empire)**: after Tiber Septim's conquests, gold coins came to be called "septims" or "drakes" from the markings the Third Empire minted on every coin: Tiber's face on one side and the red dragon on the other. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Currency
- **Third Era, Daggerfall period (3E 417)**: Emperor's Day (30 Frostfall, the Emperor's birthday) was "once the most popular holiday of the year" but its traditions had "fallen into neglect." — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Daggerfall:Holidays
- **3E 427 (Morrowind period)**: outside the official channels run by the East Empire Company, the Empire bans dealing in moon sugar, skooma and Dwemer artifacts, so smuggling is big business on Vvardenfell. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Vvardenfell
- **3E 427**: Morrowind provincial law bans moon sugar and skooma. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Crime
- **3E 433**: the Septim bloodline ends with Martin Septim during the Oblivion Crisis. The coins keep the Septim names afterward. — [unverified] (Lore:Martin_Septim); the continued names are supported by https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Currency ("by the Third and Fourth Eras… septims or drakes")
- **4E 5 (Red Year)**: Red Mountain erupts. This sits outside this area's searches. — [unverified] (Lore:Red_Year)
- **4E 6**: Argonian invasion of southern Morrowind. — [unverified] (Lore:Morrowind)
- **c. 4E 17**: Titus Mede I takes the Ruby Throne, ending the Stormcrown Interregnum. This bears on Emperor's Day observance. — [unverified] (Lore:Titus_Mede_I)
- **4E 98 – 4E 100 (Void Nights)**: Masser and Secunda vanish from the sky for two full years. When they return in 4E 100 the Thalmor claim credit, saying they used previously unknown "Dawn Magicks." Whether they restored the moons or only predicted their return is unclear. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Void_Nights
- **4E 98 – 4E 100**: the Khajiit see the Thalmor as saviours, which wins the Thalmor great influence in Elsweyr. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Void_Nights ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr
- **4E 115**: the Khajiit are persuaded to ally once more with the Aldmeri Dominion. — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Elsweyr ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Great_War_(book)
- **4E 175**: the White-Gold Concordat bans Talos worship. This matters for Long Chronicle religious holidays only. — [unverified] (Lore:White-Gold_Concordat)
- **4E 201, 17 Last Seed**: Skyrim begins. This is the only well-documented weekday anchor in the 4E (see §5). — Source: https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Time ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Calendar

## 2. Places (status in 4E 4, 4E 5, 4E 6, and later if known)
_This area is cross-cutting. Only places where a calendar, trade or travel fact is tied to a location are listed here._

### Vvardenfell / Bitter Coast (Morrowind)
- Controlling faction / ruler: Imperial Morrowind. The East Empire Company runs the official trade channels. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Vvardenfell
- Economy & signature goods: an official (EEC) trade in restricted goods, and a large smuggling economy in moon sugar, skooma and Dwemer artifacts. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Vvardenfell
- Smuggling route: moon sugar is most commonly smuggled in through caves on the Bitter Coast. Among bandits it is often used as currency. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Moon_Sugar ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma
- Skooma is usually smuggled together with moon sugar. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma
- There is a named smuggler den, Smuggler's Island. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Smuggler's_Island
- What happens 4E 4–6: the Red Year (4E 5) devastates Vvardenfell. The fate of the smuggling coves is unknown. — [unverified] (Lore:Red_Year)
- Atmosphere: Khajiit merchants are the only buyers of moon sugar: Ajira and Ra'Virr in Balmora, Baissa and J'Rasha in Vivec. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Moon_Sugar
- Sources: see bullets.

### Velothi strongholds (Propylon network, Vvardenfell)
- Controlling faction / ruler: ruined ancient Dunmer fortresses. — [unverified] (Morrowind:Propylon_Chambers)
- Travel: the Propylon Chambers are one of Morrowind's transport systems. UESP's transport page gives them their own section. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Transport
- There are ten chambers in a ring (Andasreth, Berandas, Falasmaryon, Falensarano, Hlormaren, Indoranyon, Marandus, Rotheran, Telasero, Valenvaryon). Each link needs its own Propylon Index. — [unverified] (Morrowind:Propylon_Chambers)

- 4E 4–6: unknown. Strongholds on the ash-blasted north and west coasts are likely affected by the Red Year. — [unverified]

## 3. Factions & people (who, stance, fate)
- **East Empire Company**: holds the official channels for otherwise-banned goods (moon sugar, skooma, Dwemer artifacts) on Vvardenfell. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Vvardenfell
- **Mages Guild**: does not take part in the Merchants' Festival, so its prices stay high. Most people who need a magic item wait two months for Tales and Tallows. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Daggerfall:Holidays
- **Mages Guild**: on Tales and Tallows (3 Hearthfire) it "completely thrives." All magical items are half price "in celebration of the oldest magical science, necromancy." — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Daggerfall:Holidays ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Tales_and_Tallows
- **Mages Guild**: runs the Guild Guide teleport service between guildhalls in Morrowind. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Transport (section exists); details [unverified]
- **Mages Guild**: dissolved at some point in the 4E, before 4E 201. The exact date and whether it was still active in 4E 4–6 are unknown. — [unverified] (Lore:Mages_Guild)
- **Imperial Cult**: Divine Intervention teleports the caster to an Imperial Cult shrine. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Transport
- **Tribunal Temple**: Almsivi Intervention teleports the caster to the nearest Tribunal Temple. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Transport
- **Tribunal Temple + House Indoril**: jointly hold the Festival of Forbearance ("Fast of Forebearance"), a days-long holiday honouring Almalexia, the "Healing Mother." Bards play, and some priests wear a crown modelled on her ceremonial war mask. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Holidays
- **Temples (generic)**: on First Planting (7 First Seed) and Second Planting (7 Second Seed) clerics run free clinics. Second Planting is the year's second and last free clinic. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Holidays
- **Temples (generic)**: on Mid Year Celebration (16 Mid Year) blessings cost half the usual donation, "perhaps" to soften the yearly news of the Emperor's latest tax increase. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Holidays
- **Temples (generic)**: on North Wind's Prayer (15 Evening Star) every service (blessing, curing, healing) costs half the usual donation. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Daggerfall:Holidays
- **Thieves Guild**: uses the chaos of Jester's Day (28 Rain's Hand) to pick pockets. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Holidays
- **Thalmor**: claimed to have restored the moons in 4E 100 using "Dawn Magicks." — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Void_Nights
- **Polydor & Eloisa**: the Lovers of Heart's Day. UESP says the people were real but the legend is fantasy, spun to mythologise a true and tragic story. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Heart's_Day ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Legend_of_the_Lovers
- **Pelinal Whitestrake**: split the Ayleid Midyear month. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Calendar ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Pelinal_Whitestrake
- **Tiber Septim**: his face is on the obverse of the septim. His birthday is Tibedetha (24 Mid Year, Alcaire). — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Currency ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Tiber_Septim
- **Frandar Hunding**: Redguard leader honoured by the festival Dirij Tereur. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Redguard
- **Sheor**: ancient Breton god of crop failure, appeased at the Breton Harvest Festival. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Holidays
- **Daedric summoning days (verified ones)**:
  - Azura: Hogithum, 21 First Seed (artifact: Azura's Star). — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Holidays ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Daggerfall:Holidays
  - Malacath: 8 Frostfall. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Malacath

## 4. Goods, trade & laws relevant to this area (what is produced, what is contraband, tariffs, currency)

### Currency
- Tamriel's common currency is gold coin. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Gold ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Currency
- **Septims / drakes**: by the Third and Fourth Eras, gold coins were commonly called septims or drakes after the markings the Third Empire minted on all coins: Tiber Septim's face on one side and the red dragon on the other. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Currency
- The official name is "Septims," but "gold" is the everyday term. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Gold
- "Drakes" is slang for the same coin. — https://forums.uesp.net/viewtopic.php?f=30&t=38345 (forum); https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Currency
- During the Stormcrown Interregnum (4E 4–6), the coins in circulation still bear Tiber Septim's face, even though no Septim sits on the throne. — derived from https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Currency ; minting policy after 3E 433 is [unverified]
- Before Tiber Septim, the coins were not "septims." ESO-era (2E 582) gold is simply "gold." — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Gold
- Other coinage, such as Dwemer coins, ancient Nordic and Ayleid coins, and Akaviri/Tsaesci coins, exists mainly as curios or loot, not as current money. — [unverified] (Lore:Currency)

### Banks, credit & moneylending
- Banks can offer loans or letters of credit denominated in gold. These promissory notes are sometimes called a **"writ of coin."** — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Currency
- Daggerfall-era (3E 417) Iliac Bay banks offered deposits, loans with a repayment deadline (default brings legal trouble), and letters of credit that turn heavy gold into paper. — [unverified] (Daggerfall:Banks)
- Gold has weight in Daggerfall (roughly 400 coins per kg), which is the in-world reason letters of credit exist. Later games treat gold as weightless. — [unverified] (Daggerfall:Gold)
- No search result described a Third-Empire mint, an Imperial bank, or how moneylending worked in 4E Morrowind. Treat any such institution in the game as a suggestion. — (gap noted)

### Contraband & trade law
- **Imperial ban**: outside EEC channels, dealing in moon sugar, skooma and Dwemer artifacts is banned (Vvardenfell). — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Vvardenfell
- **Provincial ban**: Morrowind law names moon sugar and skooma as the two banned substances. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Crime
- **Enforcement in practice (Morrowind)**: simply possessing them is not technically a crime, because no one reports it. The real effect is that most merchants refuse to trade with you while you carry them. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Crime
- **Exception**: Khajiit merchants buy moon sugar. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Moon_Sugar
- **Moon sugar** is a potent narcotic, "illegal in Morrowind and the rest of the Empire." — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Drugs
- **Skooma** is a hallucinatory narcotic refined from moon sugar. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Skooma
- **Slavery**: legal in Morrowind under Imperial tolerance until the late 3E or early 4E. Abolition and its date are relevant to 4E 4–6 Argonian grievances. — [unverified] (Lore:Slavery)
- **Ebony**: a Vvardenfell ore whose mining was Imperial-chartered (e.g. the Caldera Mining Company in 3E 427). — [unverified] (Lore:Ebony)
- **Tariffs**: no search result gave Imperial tariff rates. — (gap noted)

### Travel magic & transport (relevant to 4E 4–6)
- **Mark**: sets a marker to return to later with Recall. Only one Mark can exist at a time. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Mark
- **Recall**: returns the caster to the Mark. Followers do not come along. It is blocked in certain places (e.g. Magas Volar, the Akulakhan chamber). — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Recall
- **Divine Intervention**: teleports the caster to an Imperial Cult shrine. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Transport
- **Almsivi Intervention**: teleports the caster to the nearest Tribunal Temple. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Transport
- Intervention spells work out "nearest" from your last exterior location, not your current position. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind_talk:Almsivi_Intervention (talk page)
- Common traveller's practice: combine Mark/Recall with both Intervention spells to reach most temples and shrines quickly. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Useful_Spells
- Followers cannot go through Recall, Intervention or propylon travel. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Transport
- **Guild Guides** (Mages Guild teleport between guildhalls) and **Propylon Chambers** are separate transport systems. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Transport
- Guild Guide halls in 3E 427: Ald'ruhn, Balmora, Caldera, Sadrith Mora and Vivec; travel costs a fee. — [unverified] (Morrowind:Transport)
- Silt striders and boats are the mundane long-distance options on Vvardenfell. — [unverified] (Morrowind:Transport)
- **Levitation**: freely available in 3E 427 Morrowind (spells, potions; Telvanni towers assume it). A UESP Scroll of Icarian Flight entry exists, but its content (Tarhiel the Bosmer jumping to his death) is [unverified]. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Scrolls (page existence only)
- Levitation is not available in Cyrodiil by 3E 433 (the so-called "Levitation Act"). Date and authority are uncertain. — [unverified] (Lore:Levitation)
- Mysticism as a school (which holds Mark, Recall and the Interventions) seems to have faded by 4E 201. Whether these spells were still taught in 4E 4–6 is unknown. — [unverified] (Lore:Mysticism)
- After the Tribunal's fall (Almalexia and Sotha Sil dead, Vivec gone by 3E 427–4E), whether Almsivi Intervention still works is not established in any source found. — [unverified] (game-design question)

### Argonian months as cultural moods (Jel calendar)
- **Vakka**: tied to origins. People show extra respect to tribal elders. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Seasons_of_Argonia
- **Xeech**: "the hiding time," a month of planting seeds and ideals, melancholy, and the **First Mourning**. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Seasons_of_Argonia
- **Hist-Tsoko**: "perhaps the holiest month," given to knowledge, wisdom and fullness of potential. It holds the **Second Mourning**. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Seasons_of_Argonia
- **Thtithil-Gah**: foolishness and frivolity, a relief after Hist-Tsoko. Travelling entertainers earn most of their year's income in this month. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Seasons_of_Argonia
- **Nushmeeko**: celebrates hard, thankless, everyday work. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Argonian
- **Shaja-Nushmeeko**: a month of mystery and debate, contemplating change and shifting values. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Argonian
- **Saxhleel**: tribe members are free to follow creative passions such as pottery and woodcarving, with a pervasive sense of things coming to a close. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Argonian ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Seasons_of_Argonia
- **Xulomaht**: the **Third Mourning**, the strongest of the three because it is the literal end of the year. Also festivals honouring the dead. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:The_Seasons_of_Argonia ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Jel

### Weekdays
- Seven days, in order: **Morndas, Tirdas, Middas, Turdas, Fredas, Loredas, Sundas**. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Calendar
- The names are corruptions of real weekdays: Sundas, Morndas, Tirdas, Turdas and Fredas come from Sun, Moon, Tyr, Thor and Frigg. Middas is likely the "middle" day and Loredas likely Nordic-derived. This is out-of-universe etymology. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/General:Calendar
- **Weekday anchor**: 17 Last Seed 4E 201 is a **Sundas** (when no save was loaded before starting a new game). — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Unbound ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Skyrim:Calendar
- CONFLICT on that anchor: see §6 (Morndas and Loredas are also claimed).
- No other verified 4E weekday anchor was found. A talk page suggests anchoring all other dates to Skyrim's first day. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Calendar (talk page)

### Moons (Masser & Secunda)
- Nirn has two moons, Masser and Secunda (also called Jode and Jone). Masser is "well over twice" Secunda's size. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moons ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Masser
- In Khajiiti belief the moons' motion builds the **Lunar Lattice** ("Ja-Kha'jay" in Ta'agra), which shields Mundus from the rest of the Aurbis. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moons
- Khajiit are born in different forms depending on the phases of the moons at birth. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Moons ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Void_Nights
- **Phases (Oblivion-era evidence)**: Masser and Secunda always share the same phase. The phase changes every 3 days, through 8 phases from new to waning crescent, which gives a 24-day cycle. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Oblivion:Repairing_the_Orrery
- Other cycle figures disagree (24 vs 28 days; Morrowind INI sets Secunda's cycle at 1.2× Masser's). See CONFLICT in §6. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Moons (talk page); https://forums.uesp.net/viewtopic.php?f=36&t=40366 (forum)
- **Dark Moon** (also the Dead, Hidden, Hollow or Ghost Moon, "True Spirit of Lorkhaj," "Den of Lorkhaj"): rumoured to be the corpse of the Missing God. Azurah burned Lorkhaj's body and scattered the ashes across the Lattice. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Dark_Moon
- **Bloodmoon**: during Hircine's Great Hunt, Secunda turns deep red. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Secunda
- **Void Nights** (4E 98–100): both moons vanish (see §1). — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Void_Nights

### Race naming conventions (rules + examples)
- **General**: UESP names races as their members do in-game, e.g. "Dunmer" not "Dark Elf," "Orc" not "Orsimer." — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/UESPWiki:Lore ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Races
- **Imperial**: Latin-style given name plus family name; emperors and notables use regnal numbers. Examples: Martin Septim, Uriel Septim VII, Titus Mede, Caius Cosades, Hieronymus Lex, Adamus Phillida. — [unverified] (Lore:Imperial_Names)
- **Breton**: French- and Celtic-flavoured given names, often with surnames; royalty use a single name. Examples: Jauffre, Lysandus, Gothryd, Eadwyre, Sybille Stentor, Belethor. — [unverified] (Lore:Breton_Names)
- **Redguard**: names are built from prefix + vowel + suffix and are unisex. Redguards have no surnames. Derivation prefixes carry meaning: **al-** = "comes from (a place)", **at-** = "direct descendant of". — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Redguard_Names
  - Examples: Frandar Hunding (https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Redguard); Cyrus, Iszara, Kematu, Saadia, Nazeem [unverified].
- **Nord**: given name plus a family/clan name, a "-son"/"-daughter" patronymic, or an earned epithet. Examples: Ulfric Stormcloak, Balgruuf the Greater, Olfrid Battle-Born, Vignar Gray-Mane, Maven Black-Briar, Hrisskar Flat-Foot. — [unverified] (Lore:Nord_Names)
- **Dunmer**: given name plus a house or family surname (Great House rulers may put the House first, as in "Hlaalu Helseth"). Ashlanders often use tribal or descriptive forms. Examples: Athyn Sarethi, Bolvyn Venim, Dram Bero, Divayth Fyr, Nibani Maesa, Sul-Matuul. — [unverified] (Lore:Dunmer_Names)
- **Altmer**: usually a single flowing given name; surnames are rare and noble clans are named separately (e.g. Direnni). Examples: Elenwen, Ancano, Ondolemar, Calcelmo, Faralda, Ocato. — [unverified] (Lore:Altmer_Names)
- **Mer ethnonyms** follow the "-mer" pattern: Aldmer = "First Folk", Chimer = "Changed Folk". — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Mer
- **Bosmer**: Arena and Daggerfall names use prefix + suffix pairs, with nature-word surnames (prefixes Blue, Fern, Forest, Ivy, Moss, Night, Oak, Pine, River). — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Bosmer_Names
  - Examples: Fargoth, Faendal, Anoriath, Elrindir, Malborn, Niruin [unverified].
- **Orc**: family names are usually (not always) prefixed **gro-** (male) or **gra-** (female), followed by the clan or stronghold name. Morrowind and Online have exceptions. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Orc_Names
  - Examples: Gortwog gro-Nagorm, Yamarz gro-Shub, Urag gro-Shub, Ghorza gra-Bagol, Mazoga [unverified].
- **Khajiit**: given names usually describe a talent or skill. Honorifics normally come before the name, separated by an apostrophe (rarely a hyphen or nothing), and can shrink to one letter (e.g. "J'", "M'"). — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiiti_Honorifics
  - Honorific meanings: **dar** is perhaps the oldest, for those "nimble in fingers and wits" (thief, clever). **ra** is for an esteemed leader of soldiers, trade or governance. **ri** is rarely given and reserved for great leaders. **dro** also appears. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiiti_Honorifics ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names
  - "Dar" is the only title known to work as a suffix. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Khajiit_Names (talk page)
  - Family names appeared in Arena and Daggerfall, were dropped after Redguard, and reappeared briefly in Legends. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names
  - Arena/Daggerfall male prefixes: Ab', Ak', Akh', Am, Fa', Hus, Mo, Moham, Moj, Na, Om, Sha, Sin, Za', Zan'. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Khajiit_Names
  - Examples: Ra'Virr, J'Rasha, Ajira (Morrowind merchants, https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Morrowind:Moon_Sugar); Ri'saad, M'aiq, J'zargo [unverified].
- **Argonian**: two kinds of name. Traditional **Jel** names are received at a naming rite, and **Tamrielic** names are often translations of them: Haj-Ei ("Haj" = hidden, "Ei" = eye/eyes) becomes "Hides His Eyes". Jel is said to come from the Hist. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Argonian_Names ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Jel
  - Useful Jel name parts: raj = elder, beeko = friend, Nujei = sister. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Argonian_Names ; https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore:Jel
  - Oddity: J'Ram-Dar, an Argonian in Morrowind, carries a Khajiit-pattern name. — https://en.uesp.net/wiki/Lore_talk:Argonian_Names (talk page)
  - Examples: Haj-Ei / Hides-His-Eyes (verified above); Gulum-Ei, Talen-Jei, Huleeya, Wujeeta, Deeja [unverified].
